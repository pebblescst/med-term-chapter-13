Chapter 13 — Two Special Senses
👁 The Eye
Vision
Light enters eye → focused on retina → converted to nerve impulse → optic nerve → occipital cortex
Refraction
Bending of light waves as they pass through cornea and lens to focus on retina
Accommodation
Lens changes shape to focus on near/far objects; ciliary muscle controls lens curvature
Ophthalmology (ophthalm/o) — eye specialty · Ophthalmologist (MD) · Optometrist (OD) · Optician (lens fitting)
👂 The Ear
Hearing
Sound waves → external ear → eardrum vibrates → ossicles amplify → cochlea converts to nerve impulse → auditory nerve → temporal cortex
Equilibrium/Balance
Vestibular system in inner ear (semicircular canals + otolith organs) detects movement and gravity
Eustachian tube
Connects middle ear to nasopharynx; equalizes pressure; route for middle ear infections
Otolaryngology / ENT (ot/o = ear + laryng/o + -logy) · Otolaryngologist · Audiologist (hearing testing/rehab)

Eye Anatomy — Light Pathway & Structures
1
Corneacorne/o, kerat/o
Transparent anterior dome; first and strongest refracting surface (~2/3 of total refractive power). Avascular — nourished by aqueous humor. Keratitis = inflammation of cornea.
2
Aqueous humor
Clear watery fluid filling anterior and posterior chambers; produced by ciliary body; drains via trabecular meshwork (Canal of Schlemm). Blockage → ↑ intraocular pressure → glaucoma.
3
Irisir/o, irid/o
Colored diaphragm with circular and radial smooth muscles; controls pupil size. Dilates (mydriasis) in dim light; constricts (miosis) in bright light. Cycl/o = ciliary body.
4
Pupilpupill/o, cor/o, core/o
Opening in center of iris through which light passes. Size controlled by iris muscles. coreoplasty = surgical repair of pupil.
5
Lensphac/o, phak/o
Biconvex transparent structure; provides fine focus (accommodation). Ciliary muscle changes shape. aphakia (a- + phak/o) = absence of lens. Cataract = clouding of lens.
6
Vitreous humorvitre/o
Gel-like substance filling posterior cavity; maintains eye shape; does not regenerate. Floaters = debris in vitreous. vitrectomy = surgical removal.
7
Retinaretin/o
Light-sensitive inner layer; contains photoreceptors: Rods (dim light, peripheral, black/white) and Cones (color, central, daytime). Macula lutea (macul/o) = central zone; fovea = center of macula — highest cone density (sharpest vision). Optic disk = blind spot where optic nerve exits.
Coats of the Eye — 3 Layers
Fibrous coat (outer)
Sclera (scler/o) — "white of eye"; tough protective outer layer. Cornea — anterior transparent portion of fibrous coat.
Vascular coat / Uvea (uve/o)
Choroid (choroid/o) — pigmented, vascular; nourishes retina. Ciliary body (cycl/o) — produces aqueous humor; lens focus. Iris (ir/o, irid/o) — controls pupil.
Nervous coat (inner)
Retina (retin/o) — photoreceptors; rods and cones; visual signals to optic nerve.
Accessory Structures
Orbit — bony socket protecting the eye
Eyelids (blephar/o, palpebr/o) — protect eye; blink reflex; palpebral fissure = opening between lids
Conjunctiva (conjunctiv/o) — thin mucous membrane lining eyelids and covering sclera
Lacrimal gland (lacrim/o, dacry/o) — produces tears (lacrimation); lubricates and cleanses
Meibomian gland — sebaceous glands in eyelids; produce oily layer of tear film
Extraocular muscles — 6 muscles move the eye in all directions
Refraction: light bends as it passes through cornea → aqueous → lens → vitreous → retina. Emmetropia = perfect vision (normal refraction).
Ear Anatomy — Sound Pathway & Three Divisions
1
External Earot/o, aur/i
Auricle/pinna (aur/i) — collects sound waves. External auditory canal (EAC) — channels sound to eardrum; lined with ceruminous glands (produce cerumen/earwax). Cerumen protects and lubricates.
2
Tympanic membrane (eardrum)myring/o, tympan/o
Thin membrane separating external from middle ear; vibrates when struck by sound waves. Perforated eardrum → conductive hearing loss. myringotomy = incision; tympanoplasty = repair.
3
Middle Ear — Ossiclesossicul/o
Three tiny bones (smallest in the body) that amplify and transmit vibrations: Malleus (hammer) → Incus (anvil) → Stapes (stirrup). Stapes footplate vibrates against oval window. Eustachian tube (salping/o) connects to nasopharynx; equalizes pressure.
4
Inner Ear — Cochleacochle/o
Snail-shaped, fluid-filled structure. Oval window vibration → fluid waves → stimulate hair cells (organ of Corti) → convert mechanical to electrical signals → cochlear nerve (CN VIII). Frequency (pitch) detected along cochlear length.
5
Inner Ear — Vestibular systemlabyrinth/o
Balance and equilibrium. Semicircular canals — detect rotational movement (3 planes). Utricle & saccule — otolith organs; detect linear acceleration and gravity. Labyrinthitis = inflammation; vertigo from vestibular dysfunction.
The 3 Ossicles — Memory: MIS
Malleus (hammer)
Attached to tympanic membrane; first ossicle; receives vibration from eardrum
Incus (anvil)
Middle ossicle; transmits vibration from malleus to stapes
Stapes (stirrup)
Smallest bone in the body; footplate connects to oval window of cochlea
Memory: MIS = Malleus → Incus → Stapes. Or "Hammer → Anvil → Stirrup"
Hearing Loss Types
Conductive hearing loss
Problem in external or middle ear; sound not conducted to inner ear. Causes: cerumen impaction, otitis media, perforated eardrum, otosclerosis. Treatable.
Sensorineural hearing loss (SNHL)
Damage to cochlear hair cells or auditory nerve (CN VIII). Causes: aging (presbycusis), noise exposure, ototoxic drugs. Usually permanent.
Mixed hearing loss
Combined conductive and sensorineural components.
Presbycusis (presby- = old + acous/o = hearing) — age-related sensorineural hearing loss; most common cause of hearing loss in adults.
Cataract
Clouding/opacity of the crystalline lens. phac/o, phak/o. Most common cause of reversible blindness worldwide. Causes: aging (most common), UV exposure, diabetes, trauma, congenital. Treatment: surgical lens extraction + IOL (intraocular lens) implant.
Glaucoma
Elevated intraocular pressure (IOP) → optic nerve damage → irreversible peripheral vision loss ("tunnel vision"). ton/o = tone/tension. Diagnosed by tonometry (ton/o + -metry). Open-angle (most common, gradual) vs. angle-closure (acute, painful emergency). Treatment: eye drops, laser, surgery.
Conjunctivitis ("pink eye")
Inflammation of conjunctiva. conjunctiv/o + -itis. Viral (most common, adenovirus — watery discharge, highly contagious), bacterial (purulent discharge, Staph/Strep), allergic (itching, bilateral). Neonatal (ophthalmia neonatorum) from Chlamydia/Gonorrhea.
Keratitis
Inflammation of cornea. kerat/o + -itis. Causes: HSV (dendritic ulcers — treat with antivirals), bacterial (contact lens), fungal, acanthamoeba. Symptoms: pain, photophobia, decreased vision.
Diabetic retinopathy
Retinal damage from chronic hyperglycemia. retin/o + -pathy. Leading cause of blindness in working-age adults. Non-proliferative → proliferative (new fragile vessels → vitreous hemorrhage). Treatment: laser photocoagulation, anti-VEGF injections, vitrectomy.
Age-related macular degeneration (ARMD)
Degeneration of macula (macul/o) → central vision loss; peripheral vision preserved. Dry (atrophic, 85%) or Wet (neovascular, faster/worse, anti-VEGF treatment). Leading cause of blindness in elderly.
Blepharoptosis
Drooping of the upper eyelid. blephar/o + -ptosis (drooping). Congenital or acquired (3rd nerve palsy, Horner syndrome, myasthenia gravis). blepharedema = eyelid swelling (blephar/o + -edema).
Dacryoadenitis
Inflammation of the lacrimal gland. dacry/o + aden/o + -itis. Causes: viral (EBV), bacterial, sarcoidosis. Pain and swelling over lacrimal gland (outer upper eyelid). xerophthalmia = dry eye disease (xer/o = dry + ophthalm/o).
Myopia (nearsightedness)
Near objects clear, far objects blurry. Light focuses in FRONT of retina; eyeball too long. myop/o. Corrected with concave (diverging) lenses. LASIK reshapes cornea.
Hyperopia (farsightedness)
Far objects clearer, near blurry. Light focuses BEHIND retina; eyeball too short. Corrected with convex (converging) lenses.
Presbyopia
Age-related loss of accommodation (near vision). presby- (old/elder) + -opia (vision). Lens hardens → can't change shape → difficulty focusing on near objects. Treated with reading glasses (+lenses).
Amblyopia ("lazy eye")
Reduced vision in one eye due to abnormal visual development in childhood. ambly/o (dull) + -opia. CNS suppresses input from weaker eye. Treat early (patching stronger eye, corrective lenses). ambly/o = dull/dim.
Diplopia
Double vision. dipl/o (double) + -opia. Monocular or binocular. Causes: strabismus, cranial nerve palsy (3rd, 4th, 6th), myasthenia gravis.
Hemianopsia
Loss of vision in half the visual field. hemi- (half) + an- (without) + -opsia (vision). Bitemporal hemianopsia from pituitary tumor compressing optic chiasm.
Achromatopsia
Complete color blindness. a- (without) + chromat/o (color) + -opsia. Rare; inability to distinguish any colors; from cone photoreceptor dysfunction.
Strabismus / Exotropia
Strabismus: misalignment of eyes (trop/o = turning). Exotropia: outward deviation (ex- + trop/o + -ia). Esotropia: inward (cross-eyed). Exophthalmia (exo- + ophthalm/o + -ia): abnormal protrusion of eyeball (from Graves disease/hyperthyroidism).
Otitis media (OM)
Middle ear infection. ot/o + -itis. Most common bacterial infection in children. Acute OM: fever, ear pain, bulging red eardrum; fluid behind eardrum. Causes: S. pneumoniae, H. influenzae, M. catarrhalis. Treatment: amoxicillin. Chronic OM → tympanostomy tubes (PE tubes).
Otitis externa ("swimmer's ear")
External ear canal infection. ot/o + -itis. Usually Pseudomonas or Staph; from moisture. Tragus tenderness on palpation. Treatment: topical antibiotic/steroid ear drops (ciprofloxacin + dexamethasone).
Otosclerosis
Abnormal bone remodeling → stapes becomes fixed (ankylosed) at oval window → conductive hearing loss. ot/o + scler/o + -osis. Most common cause of conductive hearing loss in young adults. Hereditary. Treatment: stapedectomy.
Presbycusis
Age-related sensorineural hearing loss. presby- (old/elder) + acous/o (hearing). Progressive bilateral high-frequency loss; from cochlear hair cell degeneration. Most common hearing loss in older adults. Treatment: hearing aids.
Ménière disease
Inner ear disorder with episodic vertigo, fluctuating sensorineural hearing loss, tinnitus, and aural fullness. From excess endolymph (endolymphatic hydrops). Treatment: low-sodium diet, diuretics, steroid injections.
Tinnitus
Ringing, buzzing, or humming in the ears without external sound source. Symptom of many ear conditions: noise exposure, ototoxic drugs (aspirin, aminoglycosides, quinine), Ménière disease. Can be pulsatile (vascular) or non-pulsatile.
Mastoiditis
Infection of mastoid air cells (mastoid/o + -itis); complication of untreated otitis media. Post-auricular pain/swelling, displaced pinna. Risk: meningitis, brain abscess. Treatment: IV antibiotics ± mastoidectomy.
Eye Procedures
Diagnostic
Ophthalmoscopy (ophthalm/o + -scopy)
Visual examination of interior of eye with ophthalmoscope; examines retina, optic disk, macula, blood vessels; detects diabetic retinopathy, glaucoma, papilledema
Tonometry (ton/o + -metry)
Measurement of intraocular pressure (IOP); screens for glaucoma; normal IOP: 10–21 mmHg; air-puff or applanation tonometer
Visual acuity (VA) testing
Snellen chart; measures sharpness of vision; 20/20 = normal; 20/200 = legal blindness
Goniotomy (goni/o + -tomy)
goni/o = angle; incision into anterior chamber angle; treats congenital glaucoma by opening trabecular meshwork to improve drainage
Therapeutic
Keratoplasty (kerat/o + -plasty)
Corneal transplant; surgical replacement of diseased cornea with donor tissue; full-thickness (penetrating keratoplasty) or partial (DSEK/DALK)
Coreoplasty (cor/o + -plasty)
Surgical repair/reshaping of the pupil; treats pupil abnormalities, trauma, or prior surgery complications
Vitrectomy (vitre/o + -ectomy)
Surgical removal of vitreous humor; treats diabetic retinopathy, retinal detachment, vitreous hemorrhage, macular hole
LASIK (corneal reshaping) Cataract extraction + IOL Retinal laser photocoagulation Anti-VEGF injections (ARMD)
Ear Procedures
Diagnostic
Otoscopy (ot/o + -scopy)
Visual examination of external ear canal and tympanic membrane with otoscope; diagnoses otitis media, perforated eardrum, cerumen impaction
Audiometry (audi/o + -metry)
Measurement of hearing acuity; pure tone audiogram tests frequencies 250–8,000 Hz; determines type and degree of hearing loss
Tympanometry
tympan/o + -metry; measures eardrum compliance/mobility; detects fluid in middle ear, perforations, ossicular chain problems; not a hearing test
Otoacoustic emissions (OAE)
Measures sounds produced by cochlea; newborn hearing screening; objective test (no patient response needed); detects cochlear hair cell damage
Therapeutic
Myringotomy (myring/o + -tomy)
Incision into tympanic membrane; relieves pressure from middle ear fluid; tympanostomy tube (PE tube) inserted to keep opening patent; treats recurrent otitis media
Tympanoplasty (tympan/o + -plasty)
Surgical repair of perforated tympanic membrane (myringoplasty) and/or ossicles; restores hearing in conductive hearing loss
Cochlear implant
Electronic device bypasses damaged hair cells; electrodes stimulate cochlear nerve directly; for severe-profound SNHL; requires auditory rehabilitation
Stapedectomy (otosclerosis) Mastoidectomy (mastoiditis) Hearing aids (SNHL)
